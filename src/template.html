Between 5 and 11 August 2026 the Mu River flooded a chain of townships across central Sagaing after releases from Thapanseik Dam met two weeks of monsoon rain. This dashboard places 52 preserved Facebook reports onto MIMU administrative geography, keeps every claim attached to the account that made it, and shows where the sources contradict each other rather than averaging them away.

Read this as source-level reporting, not a verified situation report. Nothing here is independently confirmed. Death tolls in this collection range from 7 to 20 depending on the outlet, the date and the geographic scope being counted. A township shown without reports was not covered by any post in this collection — that is not evidence it was unaffected.

Method, data and limits

Provenance
GIS layers, all from MIMU
Township, district and region boundaries
MIMU v9.4, 1:250,000. 37 Sagaing townships. P-codes carried through so every join is by code, not by name.
Village points
MIMU PCode v9.7. 5,725 points in Sagaing; the 2,477 falling in townships with reported flooding are drawn.
Town points
MIMU v9.4, 50 towns in Sagaing, used for labels and for the DMH gauge-station locations.
Hydrography
MIMU river network (water-surface polygons, from which the Mu, Chindwin and Ayeyarwady are named) plus the 250k centreline network. Reservoirs from Lakes and Dams in Myanmar; Thapanzeik dam from Hydropower Dam Locations.
Roads, bridges, hospitals
MIMU Road Network 2022 (main and secondary only), Bridges >180 ft, and Public Hospitals 2020, each clipped to Sagaing.

Retrieved from the MIMU GeoNode GeoServer WFS in EPSG:4326, clipped to Sagaing, simplified to roughly 65 m for boundaries and 110 m for hydrography, and rounded to 4 decimal places.

How severity was assigned
Critical
Deaths reported in the township, or near-township-wide inundation with villages counted in the dozens.
Severe
Widespread inundation and mass displacement reported, without a township-specific death toll.
Moderate
Flooding reported but localised, or the township appears in affected-area lists without detail.
River watch
A gauge at or above danger level, or inside the forecast corridor, with no inundation reported. A different state, not a lower grade of flooding — which is why it sits off the severity ramp.
No reports
No post in this collection covered the township. Absence of evidence only.

Classes were assigned from the reported content of the posts, and every township's evidence basis and supporting post numbers are shown in the table and the map panel.

Known limits
Coverage follows attention, not impact
Shwebo appears in 19 posts and Ayadaw in 4. That gap measures where cameras and connectivity were, not where the water was deepest. Townships in the north of the region are barely represented.
Figures are not additive
Village and population counts come from different outlets on different dates covering overlapping areas. Summing them would double-count heavily.
Severity is editorial
The five classes are a reading of the reports, not a measurement. A satellite-derived flood extent would give a different and more defensible map.
Nothing is verified
Several posts carry political framing and some make contested allegations about the dam release. They are preserved as claims attributed to their authors, and comments disputing them are noted where the raw dataset recorded them.
The window closes mid-event
Collection stopped on 11 August while the Chindwin was still rising and a 150–245 mm forecast covered the following week. Wetlet was still rising at the cut-off.
